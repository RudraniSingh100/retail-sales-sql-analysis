# Retail Sales Analysis with SQL

Ten business questions answered with SQL (joins, CTEs, window functions) on a relational retail database: customers, products, orders and order line items.

> **Data note:** the dataset is **synthetic**, generated by `generate_data.py` with a fixed random seed (9,000 orders, 3,000 customers, 60 products, Jan 2024 to Dec 2025). It contains no real customer data. The findings below show the analysis method on this data. They are not claims about a real business.

## Tech
SQL (MySQL 8), Python (data generation + SQLite verification), window functions (`LAG`, `RANK`, `NTILE`), CTEs, multi-table joins, aggregation.

## Database design
```
customers (customer_id PK, region, segment, signup_date)
products  (product_id PK, category, unit_price, unit_cost)
orders    (order_id PK, customer_id FK, order_date, order_month, channel, discount)
order_items (item_id PK, order_id FK, product_id FK, quantity)
```
Revenue per line = `quantity * unit_price * (1 - discount)`; profit = revenue minus `quantity * unit_cost`.

## Repository layout
```
generate_data.py            creates the CSVs in data/
data/                       customers, products, orders, order_items (CSV)
sql/01_schema.sql           MySQL schema with keys and indexes
sql/02_load_data.sql        LOAD DATA statements
sql/03_analysis_queries.sql the 10 analysis queries
run_queries_sqlite.py       runs all queries without MySQL, writes results/
results/                    Q1.csv ... Q10.csv
```

## How to run
**MySQL:** run `sql/01_schema.sql`, then `sql/02_load_data.sql` (start the client with `--local-infile=1`), then `sql/03_analysis_queries.sql`.

**No MySQL needed:**
```
python generate_data.py
python run_queries_sqlite.py
```

## Questions and findings (on the synthetic data)
| # | Question | Technique | Finding |
|---|----------|-----------|---------|
| 1 | Headline numbers | multi-table join, aggregates | 9,000 orders, 2,682 active customers, revenue 7.77M, profit 2.20M (28.3% margin), average order value 863.65 |
| 2 | Monthly trend | CTE + `LAG` | Revenue grew 5.7% from 2024 (3.78M) to 2025 (3.99M). November and December are about 20% of each year's revenue, against 16.7% if spread evenly |
| 3 | Category revenue and margin | `GROUP BY` | Electronics is 65% of revenue and Furniture 27%. Office Supplies has the best margin (32.9%) but only 166K revenue |
| 4 | Top 10 products | `RANK` | 9 of the top 10 products are Electronics or Furniture, led by Electronics Item 2 |
| 5 | Region and segment | multi-key `GROUP BY` | East Consumer is the largest cell (1.54M). Average order value is about 1,050 in the East vs about 700 in the West and South for Consumers |
| 6 | Revenue concentration | `NTILE` deciles | The top 10% of customers bring 34.7% of revenue and the top 30% bring 66.8%. The bottom 50% bring 14.6% |
| 7 | Repeat vs one-time buyers | CTE + `CASE` | 78.6% of customers are repeat buyers and spend about 4.4x more (3,473 vs 787) than one-time buyers |
| 8 | Online vs store | `GROUP BY` | Online has more orders (4,898 vs 4,102), but average order value and margin are almost identical |
| 9 | Do discounts pay off? | `GROUP BY` discount tier | Margin falls from 30.8% with no discount to 13.5% at a 20% discount |
| 10 | Best category per region | `RANK() OVER (PARTITION BY ...)` | By units: Electronics in the East, Furniture in the North, Clothing in the South, Office Supplies in the West |

## Suggested actions (illustrative)
1. Protect the top customer deciles, since a small group drives a third of revenue.
2. Review the 20% discount tier, because its margin is less than half the full-price margin.
3. Plan stock and promotions around the November and December peak.
4. Find out why average order value in the West and South trails the East.

## Verification
Totals from the SQL queries (revenue, profit, average order value) were cross-checked against an independent pandas calculation and match exactly.
